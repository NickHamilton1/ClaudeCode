# ABC Preschool — Prospective Parent Research

**Audience:** Designer/developer (Nick) building the ABC Preschool site (ministry of First Baptist Church of Concord, NC).
**Purpose:** Surface what real prospective parents search for, worry about, and need so feature, copy, and IA decisions can be prioritized off evidence rather than instinct.
**Date:** 2026-04-27
**Note on sourcing:** Where forum posts were behind login walls or 403'd to fetch (Quora, BabyCenter, Mothering Forum threads), I cite the canonical thread URL and paraphrase what indexed snippets show. Where I could fetch, I quote directly. Reddit indexing has degraded since their API changes; the strongest direct quote material in this report comes from The Bump, MetaFilter, daycare.com forums, Berkeley Parents Network, and Well-Trained Mind forums.

## 1. TOP SEARCH QUERIES

These are queries parents actually type when researching preschools, clustered by intent. Sources: Google "People Also Ask" / autocomplete patterns, parenting forum thread titles, and the SEO/keyword analyses cited below.

- `Christian preschool near me`

**Safety & care (high intent)**

**Faith-based fit (highest intent for ABC's audience)**

## 2. WHAT PARENTS WANT TO KNOW IN THE FIRST 30 SECONDS

Ranked. Sourced primarily from preschool-website best-practice writeups and what parents say is missing when they have to call.

1. **"Is this a real, licensed school near me?"** — Address, map, hours, ages served. Mobile parents "research childcare on their phones during lunch breaks and late at night, so sites must load fast" ([Emulent](https://emulent.com/resources/childcare-center-local-seo-client-story/)).
2. **Hours & schedule** — "M–F 8:45 am – 12:30 pm" with "3-day or 5-day option" needs to be visible without scrolling. Parents bounce when daily schedules are vague: "Vague or hard-to-find information about daily schedules can be a red flag" ([Just Like Angels](https://www.justlikeangelscc.com/how-childcare-websites-help-parents-understand-daily-schedules/)).
3. **Ages served** — "1-year-olds through TK" stated literally with class names.
4. **Tuition (real numbers)** — Parents "go in expecting a clear tuition rate only to discover a list of add-ons" ([Carepoint Academy](https://carepointeacademy.com/preschool-enrollment-costs/)). The current ABC page does include the $290/$385 + $100 reg fee — keep that visible.
5. **Faith identity in plain language** — Parents searching Christian preschool want to know "how much religion is brought into the classroom" before they call ([The Bump thread](https://forums.thebump.com/discussion/12368562/sending-a-secular-child-to-a-religious-preschool)).
6. **One photo of real children/teachers** — Real photos (not stock) are a primary trust signal. "People can usually tell the difference between stock photos and real photos" ([Johnny Flash Productions](https://www.johnnyflash.com/best-preschool-websites/)).
7. **Curriculum name** — ABeka should appear above the fold; parents are searching it.
8. **Clear next step** — "Schedule a tour" and "Apply" as buttons. Best-in-class sites "make it easy for parents to take the next step ... without having to pick up the phone" ([Johnny Flash](https://www.johnnyflash.com/best-preschool-websites/)).
9. **Phone number, click-to-call** — Mobile parents will tap before they read.
10. **Director's name + face** — Trust comes from a person, not a brand. Community Church Preschool is cited as exemplary because it "showcases each of the teachers and teacher's aides on staff with photos and in-depth bios" ([Johnny Flash](https://www.johnnyflash.com/best-preschool-websites/)).
11. **Availability signal** — "Mostly full but accepting applications" beats silence; a "join waitlist" signal converts urgency.

## 3. UNSPOKEN ANXIETIES & OBJECTIONS

Fears parents have but rarely articulate at the first inquiry. Quotes from real threads.

**Abuse / supervision fears.** Forum-cited red flags include: "If a provider does not allow you to drop by unannounced anytime, that is a red flag," "doors left unlocked, no visitor check-in process, outdoor play areas without fencing... no camera coverage" ([Button Law Firm — Hidden Red Flags](https://www.buttonlawfirm.com/faqs/secret-signs-of-a-bad-daycare-every-parent-should-know.cfm); [Care.com](https://www.care.com/c/8-signs-of-a-bad-day-care-center/)). Background checks are a federal CCDF requirement but the [19th News investigation](https://19thnews.org/2024/04/child-care-workers-background-checks-federal-law/) found "27 states failing to conduct at least some of the checks" — parents who've read this want to see proof.

**Separation anxiety (parent's, not just child's).** "Sometimes parents feel the separation anxiety more so than the preschooler... many feel like they're 'doing it all wrong' when their child clings to their legs, sobs for them to stay" ([Akron Children's blog](https://www.akronchildrens.org/inside/2025/08/23/preschool-prep-5-ways-to-manage-your-own-separation-anxiety-mom-and-dad/)). They want to know what drop-off looks like and that the staff has handled this 1,000 times.

**"Is my kid behind?"** Parents "can be plagued by anxieties, worrying that their child will get lost in a big classroom" ([Child Mind Institute](https://childmind.org/article/is-your-child-is-ready-for-kindergarten/)). Teachers stress "emotional maturity and independence are more important to readiness than 'pre-academic' skills like knowing letters, numbers, colors and shapes" — but parents don't know that.

**Teacher turnover.** "High turnover is a massive red flag for parents... young children who experienced high teacher turnover had fewer gains in vocabulary and literacy, as well as increased behavioral problems" ([Teaching Strategies blog](https://teachingstrategies.com/blog/the-impact-of-teacher-turnover-on-child-development-and-learning/); [Yale ECE turnover research](https://medicine.yale.edu/news-article/understanding-ece-teacher-turnover/)). Average tenure of staff is something parents wish was disclosed.

**Hidden costs.** "Many parents go in expecting a clear tuition rate only to discover a list of add-ons that weren't part of the initial discussion" ([Carepoint Academy](https://carepointeacademy.com/preschool-enrollment-costs/)). On daycare.com forums parents argue registration fees should be one-time, not annual ([daycare.com forum](https://www.daycare.com/forum/showthread.php?t=28143)).

**Religious-fit worries from secular parents.** Direct quotes from The Bump:
- "My mother had a really bad experience with religion as a child... I would have had a visceral reaction to the Christian prayers." (changingnames)
- "The biggest concern being evolution vs creationism... we plan to... fill in any holes at home." (PineApple85)
- "I wouldn't send my kids to an evangelical Christian school." (yeah.)
([The Bump thread](https://forums.thebump.com/discussion/12368562/sending-a-secular-child-to-a-religious-preschool))

**Secular-curriculum worries from devout parents.** A parallel Christian-school-website concern is "are they Christian in name only?" — addressed in [Foundation Worldview's](https://foundationworldview.com/blog/is-public-education-right-for-your-child-what-christian-parents-need-to-know) framing of why Christian parents intentionally choose faith-integrated education.

**"What if my kid doesn't fit?"** Parents fear being told their kid was kicked out, dismissed, or singled out. Discipline philosophy is a hidden anxiety and rarely on websites.

## 4. FAITH-BASED PRESCHOOL SPECIFIC CONCERNS

Beyond general daycare questions, parents researching Christian preschools ask:

**"Do we have to be members?"** — Common enough that competing church preschools answer it explicitly. Mallard Creek Presbyterian states: "MCPC Preschool students are not required to attend Mallard Creek Church" ([Mallard Creek](https://mallardcreekchurch.org/connect/preschool/)). PromiseTown Preschool publishes the same ([Fun4FirstCoast listing](https://fun4firstcoastkids.com/Education-Childcare/Preschools-and-Child-Care-Centers-Faith-Based/PromiseTown-Preschool-at-Ponte-Vedra-Presbyterian-Church/View-details)). ABC's site does not currently address this.

**"How much Bible is daily vs. weekly?"** — Practice varies wildly across church preschools and parents want it spelled out. Examples surveyed: Northwest Bible Church (Dallas) — chapel weekly, Twos through PK-4 ([Northwest Bible](https://northwestbible.org/preschool/)); Wilmington Christian Academy — chapel one morning a week ([Wilmington Christian](https://www.wilmingtonchristian.com/preschool)); Covenant Presbyterian Charlotte — monthly chapel + "blessings at snack time and age-appropriate Bible stories" ([Covenant Presbyterian](https://www.covenantpresby.org/preschool)); First Christian Academy — "Wednesdays at 8:40 am" ([FCA](https://firstchristianacademy.com/preschool/daily-schedule/)). Naming the cadence builds trust.

**"What denomination, and does it matter?"** — Baptist vs. Methodist preschools differ in tone. The denominationdifferences.com summary notes Baptists are "independent and congregational" while Methodists are "connectional"; baptism doctrine differs ([Denomination Differences](https://denominationdifferences.com/compare/baptist-vs-methodist)). For the typical non-Baptist parent shopping locally, the relevant signal is "we welcome families of any faith background, here's what your child will hear."

**"Are non-Christian families actually welcome, or just tolerated?"** — On MetaFilter and Quora, parents repeatedly ask whether their kid will "be in trouble" for not praying or whether teachers will single them out. Search returns paraphrase the recurring concern as "how the preschool deals with theological differences between families, and whether children who don't want to pray will be in trouble" ([MetaFilter](https://ask.metafilter.com/178072/Should-I-consider-a-Christian-preschool-for-my-kid-when-I-am-a-nonbeliever)).

**"Will my kid come home saying things that scare me?"** — Reported across forums: "concerned that their children may come home talking about God and Jesus" and that "young children struggle to distinguish between factual information like how plants grow versus religious teachings" ([Mothering Forum thread](https://www.mothering.com/threads/secular-family-religious-preschool-can-this-work.1356985/)).

**"Are church preschools a better deal?"** — Common positive: "Church preschools offer 'a strong start in life for about half what it would cost at a secular preschool'" ([The Way PDO](https://thewaypdo.com/preschool/10-reasons-church-preschools-are-better/)). Parents often arrive open because the price beats secular options.

**"Long-term, does this 'turn' my kid religious or away from religion?"** — Repeated reassurance across threads: "messages from immediate family are much more powerful than what teachers at school say, and religious preschool experience did not significantly influence children to become super-religious in the long term" (paraphrased finding from the [Mothering Forum thread](https://www.mothering.com/threads/secular-family-religious-preschool-can-this-work.1356985/) and [The Bump](https://forums.thebump.com/discussion/9115068/church-daycare-but-not-religious)).

## 5. WHAT MAKES PARENTS BOUNCE FROM A PRESCHOOL WEBSITE

Friction patterns documented in school-website UX writeups:

1. **No tuition listed / "call for pricing"** — "When centers hide information or make it available only after enrollment, it raises questions about transparency and builds concerns rather than confidence" ([Qrolic — outdated preschool websites](https://qrolic.com/blog/outdated-preschool-website-redesign-2026/)).
2. **Stock photos of generic kids** — "People can usually tell the difference between stock photos and real photos" ([Johnny Flash](https://www.johnnyflash.com/best-preschool-websites/)).
3. **No daily schedule** — "Quality programs have intentionally designed routines and are proud to share them" ([Just Like Angels](https://www.justlikeangelscc.com/how-childcare-websites-help-parents-understand-daily-schedules/)).
4. **Bad mobile experience** — "Parents are constantly browsing on their phones, and a poorly optimized mobile site will send them straight to competitors" ([Qrolic](https://qrolic.com/blog/outdated-preschool-website-redesign-2026/)).
5. **No clear next step** — Best-in-class sites have a single CTA (Schedule a Tour / Apply); sites that bury contact behind 3 clicks lose the parent ([Johnny Flash](https://www.johnnyflash.com/best-preschool-websites/)).
6. **No teacher photos/bios** — Trust signal absence.
7. **No accreditation/license info** — NC parents specifically know to look for the NC star rating ([NC DHHS](https://ncchildcare.ncdhhs.gov/Services/Licensing/Star-Rated-License)). Religious programs in some states are exempt from licensing ([Paper Pinecone](https://www.paperpinecone.com/blog/religious-childcare-providers-are-exempt-licensing-these-states)) — if ABC is licensed under NC, that's a deliberate trust signal worth showing.
8. **Outdated calendar/news** — "Last updated 2022" telegraphs that no one is home.
9. **Confusing nav** — Putting "Tuition," "Apply," and "Schedule a Tour" anywhere other than the top fails the most common task.
10. **Walls of text** — Above-fold should answer questions, not narrate philosophy.

## 6. WHAT MAKES PARENTS TRUST A PRESCHOOL WEBSITE

Trust signals specifically called out in the research:

- **Real teacher photos with names and short bios.** Single most-cited signal. ([Johnny Flash](https://www.johnnyflash.com/best-preschool-websites/), [Reallygooddesigns](https://reallygooddesigns.com/daycare-websites/))
- **A short director video** — humanizes the school in ways copy cannot.
- **Authentic classroom photography** (parents can sniff out staged shots). Always with proper photo release. ([Reallygooddesigns](https://reallygooddesigns.com/daycare-websites/))
- **Specific testimonials with names** — "showcase testimonials with real names and photos rather than anonymous quotes to build credibility" ([Media Search Group](https://www.mediasearchgroup.com/web/design/childcare-website/)).
- **NC Star Rating + License number** — NC's 1–5 star scale is a known shorthand for NC parents ([NC DHHS](https://ncchildcare.ncdhhs.gov/Services/Licensing/Star-Rated-License); [Carolina Parent](https://www.carolinaparent.com/north-carolina-child-care-star-rating-system-explained/)).
- **NAEYC accreditation** if held — "evaluated against research-informed standards for classroom interactions, learning environments, health and safety, and family partnerships" ([NAEYC accreditation page](https://www.naeyc.org/accreditation)).
- **Daily schedule by age group** — infant/toddler/preschool schedules differ; show the relevant one ([Just Like Angels](https://www.justlikeangelscc.com/how-childcare-websites-help-parents-understand-daily-schedules/)).
- **Tuition transparently posted** — ABC already does this; preserve it.
- **Address + embedded map** — confirms physical legitimacy.
- **Background check / safety language** — explicit copy that staff are background-checked, doors are locked, sign-in required. The "open door / drop in anytime" stance is itself a trust signal ([Button Law Firm](https://www.buttonlawfirm.com/faqs/secret-signs-of-a-bad-daycare-every-parent-should-know.cfm)).
- **Average teacher tenure stat** — "Our teachers stay an average of 7 years" disarms the turnover anxiety ([Yale ECE](https://medicine.yale.edu/news-article/understanding-ece-teacher-turnover/)).

## 7. NAVIGATION & WAYFINDING PATTERNS

**Canonical preschool sitemap (synthesized from [Pipermache](https://pipermache.com/preschool-website/), [Johnny Flash](https://www.johnnyflash.com/best-preschool-websites/), [Paper Pinecone](https://www.paperpinecone.com/blog/your-guide-perfect-daycare-or-preschool-website)):**

- Home
- About (mission, faith statement, history, accreditation/license)
- Programs / Classes (by age group)
- Curriculum (ABeka, daily schedule, chapel cadence)
- Tuition & Fees
- Calendar
- Staff / Meet the Teachers
- FAQs
- Apply / Enroll
- Schedule a Tour
- Contact

**Commonly missing that parents wish was there:**
- A real **daily schedule by age group** (most church preschools omit this).
- **Faculty bios with photos** (not just director).
- **Calendar dependencies** — when ABC follows or diverges from Cabarrus County Schools' calendar (snow days, breaks, teacher workdays).
- **Photo gallery of actual classrooms** (not the building exterior).
- **A "Considering us?" or "First time here?" page** — addresses unspoken anxieties (separation, faith intensity, what to bring).
- **Waitlist transparency** — current openings by class.

**Where parents get stuck and have to call:**
- "Do you have a spot in the [age] class for [year]?" — almost always requires a phone call because availability is rarely on websites.
- "What's your discipline policy?"
- "Are you a member-required school?"
- "Can a non-Christian family enroll?"
- "Do you provide snacks?" / "What do I pack?"
- "Is there an extended care option?" — for half-day programs, this is the #1 working-parent question.
- "Is your facility licensed by NC DCDEE?"

## 8. ABEKA-SPECIFIC PARENT QUESTIONS

ABeka generates strong opinions on both sides. Parents will Google it; the website should not pretend the brand is invisible.

**Praise (from parent reviewers):**
- "Abeka is known for being particularly advanced — some say it's an entire grade year ahead of public school students." ([HowDoIHomeschool](https://howdoihomeschool.com/abeka-academy-homeschool-reviews/))
- "Biblically based, missions focused, reading program fantastic." ([Time4Learning summary](https://www.time4learning.com/blogs/news/abeka-curriculum-pros-and-cons))
- "Materials serving as a terrific foundation for preschool and early kindergarten education." ([Peppermints and Cherries blog](https://www.peppermintsandcherries.com/blog/abeka-preschool-curriculum-review-and-teaching-tips))
- "An age appropriate curriculum for preschool that takes into consideration the differences in motor skills and cognitive abilities among preschoolers." (Abeka's own framing — [Abeka preschool page](https://www.abeka.com/preschool/))

**Criticism (developmental and pedagogical):**
- "Very rigorous and a lot of time spent in front of a video... so many worksheets and tons of repetitive stuff." (homeschool parent quoted in [HowDoIHomeschool](https://howdoihomeschool.com/abeka-academy-homeschool-reviews/))
- "Daughter struggled in the very advanced preschool program... was way too much for her." ([Time4Learning Pros and Cons](https://www.time4learning.com/blogs/news/abeka-curriculum-pros-and-cons))
- "K5 grading scale caused issues, noting their child felt inadequate at age 5, saying 'I can't get an A momma I'll just take a B.'" ([Time4Learning](https://www.time4learning.com/blogs/news/abeka-curriculum-pros-and-cons))
- "Tend to focus on detailed, rote learning rather than concepts." (Cathy Duffy review — [Cathy Duffy](https://cathyduffyreviews.com/homeschool-reviews-core-curricula/grade-level-packages-and-courses/preschool-through-grade-8/a-beka-book-curriculum))
- Kinesthetic learners "dying inside when it was time for Abeka." ([Well-Trained Mind forum](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/365636-why-is-a-beka-so-disliked/))

**Worldview critique (relevant only because curious parents will find it):**
- ABeka science teaches young-earth creationism; "Abeka's science textbooks defy the scientific consensus regarding the origins of the universe, origins of life, and evolution, teaching the Genesis creation narrative as a literal and factual account" ([Wikipedia: Abeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abeka), citing critical academic reviews).
- The 2018 University of Florida / UCF review found ABeka content "markedly simpler and less challenging than the content of comparable textbooks used in public education" (cited in [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abeka)).
- [RightingAmerica](https://rightingamerica.net/educational-malpractice-a-review-of-an-abeka-creationist-8th-grade-science-textbook/) and [TIME magazine on conservative textbooks](https://time.com/6316978/conservative-textbooks/) carry the strongest critiques — but these are 8th-grade-level concerns; for **preschool**, the kinesthetic/rigor critique is far more relevant than the creationism critique.

**What parents specifically want to know about ABeka at the preschool level:**
- Is there homework? (Parents associate ABeka with worksheets.)
- Is there testing/grading at this age?
- How much screen-time / video?
- How is hands-on play balanced with structured instruction?
- What does a typical morning look like, minute by minute?

**Honest reassurance worth writing:** ABeka at the **preschool level** is more songs/letter-recognition/Bible-stories than the worksheet-heavy reputation that comes from upper grades. Saying so directly disarms the most common Google-induced worry.

## 9. NORTH CAROLINA / CABARRUS COUNTY LOCAL CONTEXT

**Local market.** Concord Christian moms have a dense set of options; ABC is one of several church preschools competing for the same families:
- ABC Preschool (First Baptist Concord) — [fbcconcord.org/abc-preschool](https://www.fbcconcord.org/abc-preschool/)
- Concord Christian Preschool (Concord Baptist) — [concordgo.com/ccp](https://concordgo.com/ccp/) — "registration opens to current CCP students and Concord members on February 2, and opens to the public on February 9," signaling member priority — a useful contrast point ABC can lean into ("public welcome, no membership required").
- Cornerstone Preschool at Grace Church (Global Methodist) — [graceconcord.org/cornerstone-preschool](https://www.graceconcord.org/cornerstone-preschool)
- Connect Christian Preschool — [connectchristianchurch.org/connect-preschool](https://www.connectchristianchurch.org/connect-preschool/) — uses Orange Bible Curriculum (good comparison point for "how faith is taught").
- New Beginnings (St. James Catholic) — [saintjamescatholic.org/new-beginnings-preschool](https://www.saintjamescatholic.org/new-beginnings-preschool)
- Elite Preschool (secular, Concord) — [elitepreschoolconcord.com](https://www.elitepreschoolconcord.com/)

**Public/free options that affect demand:**
- NC Pre-K via Cabarrus Partnership for Children — income-based ($) and waitlisted ([Cabarrus Partnership](https://cabarruspartnership.org/preschool-applications); [McGill Learning Center NC Pre-K explainer](https://www.mcgilllearningcenter.com/nc-pre-k-application-process-in-cabarrus-county/)). To be eligible "your child must be four years old on or before August 31st... and your family gross income must be at or below 75% of the State median income."
- Mary Frances Wall Center — county-run preschool for kids with and without disabilities ([MFWC](https://mfwc.cabarrus.k12.nc.us/preschool)).
- Families First — runs immersion preschools, "historically including NC's first immersion NC Pre-K classrooms" ([Families First](https://familiesfirstcc.org/)).

**NC-specific quality cues parents look for:**
- **NC Star Rating (1–5 stars).** "1 star = meets North Carolina's minimum licensing requirements; 5 stars = meets the highest level of voluntary enhanced standards" ([NC DHHS](https://ncchildcare.ncdhhs.gov/Services/Licensing/Star-Rated-License); [Carolina Parent](https://www.carolinaparent.com/north-carolina-child-care-star-rating-system-explained/)). If ABC is religiously exempt or chooses not to participate, the page should explain why and what equivalent assurance exists.
- **Cabarrus County Schools calendar.** Local working parents structure life around CCS dates; preschools that publish "we follow CCS calendar" or call out their differences (typically half-day preschools have more breaks and end earlier) reduce phone calls.
- **Pricing context:** Care.com data for Concord lists $600–$1,500/mo for full-time programs; ABC's $290/$385 for half-day is well below the full-time market and that should be obvious to a price-comparing parent. ([Care.com Concord](https://www.care.com/preschools/concord-nc))

**Greater Charlotte cultural context:** Cabarrus is part of the Charlotte exurb belt — heavy church-attendance norm, strong school-choice culture, working/half-stay-at-home mom split. Half-day preschools serve a specific niche: SAH or part-time-working moms who want academic + faith formation but not full daycare. The website should speak to that family unambiguously.

## 10. RECOMMENDED CONTENT INVENTORY

### MUST-HAVE pages/sections

**Home (above the fold):**
- Headline: who we are in one sentence ("A half-day Christian preschool in Concord, NC, serving 1-year-olds through TK")
- Hours (8:45 am – 12:30 pm, M–F)
- Ages served
- Tuition at-a-glance + "$100 reg fee, no hidden costs"
- "Schedule a Tour" + "Apply" CTAs
- Phone (click-to-call) + address
- One real classroom photo

**About / Our Story:**
- Mission and ABC = "All Because of Christ" tagline
- That ABC is a ministry of First Baptist Church of Concord — and what that does and doesn't mean for non-member families
- Brief church history
- NC license info and any accreditation

**Programs by Age:**
- 1s, 2s, 3s, 4s, TK — separate sections with: schedule cadence (3-day vs 5-day), what a typical day looks like, what skills they'll build, photos of that age group's room.

**A Day at ABC:**
- A literal minute-by-minute breakdown for one age group (parents are starving for this — see [Just Like Angels](https://www.justlikeangelscc.com/how-childcare-websites-help-parents-understand-daily-schedules/) and [Emmanuel Lutheran's example](https://www.johnnyflash.com/best-preschool-websites/))
- When chapel happens, when free play happens, when ABeka instruction happens
- Snack, outdoor time, music/Spanish/sign language

**Our Curriculum (ABeka):**
- What ABeka is in plain language (a Christian academic curriculum)
- What it looks like at preschool age specifically (songs, stories, letter and number recognition — not 3rd-grade worksheets)
- Honest framing of why First Baptist chose it
- One paragraph addressing common questions ("Is there homework? Is there testing at this age? How much hands-on play?")

**Faith at ABC:**
- One section explicitly addressing: "How much Bible? How often is chapel? What if our family isn't religious — are we welcome?"
- Statement of welcome to families of any background, paired with honest disclosure of what kids will hear (Bible stories, prayer at meals/start/end of day, weekly chapel — whatever the actual cadence is).

**Meet Our Staff:**
- Director photo + bio + email
- Lead teacher photo + 2-sentence bio + years at ABC
- Average teacher tenure stat if favorable

**Tuition & Fees:**
- $290 / $385 / $100 reg fee, all visible
- What's included / what's not (snacks, supplies, field trips)
- Sibling discount? Payment schedule? Financial aid?

**FAQ — must address these specific questions (sourced from sections 3, 4, 7 above):**
1. Do we have to be members of First Baptist Concord?
2. Are non-Christian families welcome?
3. How much Bible/chapel is in the day?
4. Is there an extended care option for working parents?
5. What's the teacher-to-child ratio per age group?
6. Is the school NC-licensed? What's the star rating, or why not?
7. What discipline philosophy do you use?
8. What does my child need to bring? Diapers, lunch, change of clothes, nap mat?
9. Do you follow Cabarrus County Schools' calendar?
10. How does your waitlist work — what's the chance for fall enrollment?

- Photo gallery (with dates so parents see it's current)

## 11. TOP 10 SPECIFIC THINGS TO LIFT

1. **Real-photo hero with no stock images** — see [Kids at Cambridge Learning Center](https://www.kidsatcambridge.com/), praised for "100% authentic classroom photography (no stock images)" ([The Playbook Partner](https://www.theplaybookpartner.com/post/the-10-best-childcare-website-designs-in-the-world-and-what-you-can-steal-from-them)).

2. **"A Day in the Life" minute-by-minute schedule** — Emmanuel Lutheran Preschool "prominently features daily schedules" ([Johnny Flash](https://www.johnnyflash.com/best-preschool-websites/)). Go further: bullet the morning hour-by-hour for each age group.

3. **Director welcome video, 60 seconds, on the homepage** — see [Sentia Early Learning](https://www.sentia.vic.edu.au/) and [Learning Tree Childcare](https://learningtreechildcare.com/) for video-banner-as-trust-builder ([Playbook Partner](https://www.theplaybookpartner.com/post/the-10-best-childcare-website-designs-in-the-world-and-what-you-can-steal-from-them)).

4. **Teacher cards with photo + name + tenure + favorite age to teach** — [Community Church Preschool](https://www.johnnyflash.com/best-preschool-websites/) "showcases each of the teachers and teacher's aides on staff with photos and in-depth bios."

5. **Tuition calculator / clear price ladder** — [Sunshine House](https://sunshinehouse.com/) does a "tuition calculator, center finder, program breakdowns" ([Playbook Partner](https://www.theplaybookpartner.com/post/the-10-best-childcare-website-designs-in-the-world-and-what-you-can-steal-from-them)). For ABC, even a clean comparison table beats a paragraph.

6. **An explicit "Are non-members welcome?" section** — Mallard Creek Presbyterian's plain statement: "MCPC Preschool students are not required to attend Mallard Creek Church" ([Mallard Creek](https://mallardcreekchurch.org/connect/preschool/)). One sentence on the homepage closes a major decision gap.

7. **Faith cadence stated as a schedule** — borrow Northwest Bible Church's plain language: "Twos through PK-4 attend chapel weekly" ([Northwest Bible](https://northwestbible.org/preschool/)). Pair with a daily-Bible cadence ("Bible story at circle time each morning, prayer at snack, weekly chapel on Wednesdays").

8. **A photo gallery sorted by age group, with date-stamped recent photos** — [Imagination Station Childcare](https://www.imaginationstationchildcare.ca/) and [Goslings Nursery](https://www.goslingsnursery.co.uk/) show how authentic recent imagery + thematic illustrations build warmth without feeling outdated ([Playbook Partner](https://www.theplaybookpartner.com/post/the-10-best-childcare-website-designs-in-the-world-and-what-you-can-steal-from-them)).

9. **Schedule-a-tour booking widget** — best preschool sites "make it easy for parents to take action and take the next step online without having to pick up the phone" ([Johnny Flash](https://www.johnnyflash.com/best-preschool-websites/)). Calendly, Square Appointments, or similar — embedded directly.

10. **A short "Considering ABC?" or "Open House" page that addresses the unspoken anxieties head-on** — model after [Bright Horizons' open house guide](https://www.brighthorizons.com/article/parenting/parents-guide-center-preschool-open-house) but written as ABC's own answer to: "First time? Here's what to expect on a tour, here's how drop-off works, here's how separation anxiety is handled, here's what you'll pack." This is the single highest-leverage content gap most church preschool sites have.
